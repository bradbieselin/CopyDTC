# Outreach Sequence A: Free Audit Offer

---

## Email 1 — The Opener (Day 1)

**Subject line options:**
1. Quick thought on {{brand_name}}'s emails
2. {{first_name}}, spotted something in your welcome flow
3. One thing I'd change about {{brand_name}}'s copy

**Body:**

Hey {{first_name}},

I was poking around {{brand_name}}'s site and signed up for your emails. Your {{specific_product}} is a great product — but your copy isn't doing it justice.

Your {{specific_content_piece}} {{specific_observation}}. Small fix, big impact — I've seen that single change lift revenue 15-20% for similar brands.

I run CopyDTC. We write emails, landing pages, and ads for DTC brands using a process that nails your voice from day one.

Want me to put together a free audit of your {{audit_target}}? No pitch attached — just 3-5 pages of specific fixes you can use whether we work together or not.

Worth a look?

Brad

**CTA:** Reply to get a free audit

**Personalization points:**
- {{brand_name}} — their brand name
- {{first_name}} — founder or marketing lead's first name
- {{specific_product}} — a bestseller or hero product from their site
- {{specific_content_piece}} — the exact asset you reviewed (e.g., "welcome sequence," "abandoned cart email," "product page for the Revival Serum")
- {{specific_observation}} — one concrete, factual weakness (e.g., "buries the key benefit below the fold," "leads with features instead of the problem it solves," "has no urgency or reason to act now")
- {{audit_target}} — what you'll audit (e.g., "email flows," "product pages," "welcome sequence")

**Internal notes:**
Goal is a reply. The personalized observation proves we actually looked at their stuff — that's what separates this from every other cold email they get. Keep the observation specific and factual, never insulting. The free audit is low-friction because there's no call to book, just a reply.

## Email 2 — The Value Drop (Day 4)

**Subject line options:**
1. Stole this from {{competitor_brand}} for you
2. Here's the fix (no strings)
3. The {{dollar_amount}} tweak

**Body:**

Hey {{first_name}},

Didn't want to wait for a reply to share this — it's too useful to sit on.

Your {{specific_content_piece}} is missing {{specific_tactic}}. Here's exactly what I'd do:

{{one_paragraph_fix}}

I just helped a {{similar_industry}} brand make this same change to their {{content_type}}. They saw a {{specific_result}} in the first 30 days.

The free audit I mentioned would cover 5-6 fixes like this across your {{audit_scope}}. Want me to send it over?

Brad

**CTA:** Reply yes for the full audit

**Personalization points:**
- {{specific_content_piece}} — same asset or a different one you've reviewed
- {{specific_tactic}} — the missing element (e.g., "a post-purchase upsell sequence," "benefit-driven headlines on the product page," "a founder story in the welcome flow")
- {{one_paragraph_fix}} — 2-3 sentences with the actual recommendation, specific enough to be useful on its own (e.g., "Swap your first welcome email from the 10% discount to a founder story about why you started {{brand_name}}. Then move the discount to email 3 as a reward for reading. Brands in your space see 20-30% higher click-through when they lead with story over coupon.")
- {{similar_industry}} — a brand category close to theirs (e.g., "skincare," "supplements," "premium candle")
- {{content_type}} — what you helped the reference brand with
- {{specific_result}} — a real, specific metric (e.g., "22% lift in email revenue," "$14K in recovered carts," "3x the click-through rate on their welcome flow")
- {{competitor_brand}} — a competitor or aspirational brand in their space (use in subject line only)
- {{dollar_amount}} — estimated revenue impact of the fix (use in subject line only)
- {{audit_scope}} — what the full audit covers (e.g., "email program," "landing pages and product copy")

**Internal notes:**
This email gives away real value before asking for anything. The fix should be genuinely useful — if they implement it without ever replying, that's fine. It proves competence better than any claim. The case study reference adds credibility without a formal case study attachment. Keep the fix specific to their brand, not generic advice.

## Email 3 — The Breakup (Day 8)

**Subject line options:**
1. Closing the loop on {{brand_name}}
2. Last one from me
3. Not for everyone

**Body:**

Hey {{first_name}},

I'll keep this short — I know you're busy building {{brand_name}}.

I sent a couple of notes about your {{audit_target}} that I think could move the needle. If the timing's off, totally get it.

But if your copy is something you want to tighten up this quarter, the audit offer stands. Takes me about 2 hours, and you'd walk away with a prioritized list of fixes ranked by revenue impact.

Either way, no hard feelings. I'm rooting for {{brand_name}} regardless.

Brad

P.S. If this lands better in a few months, just reply "later" and I'll circle back.

**CTA:** Reply to get the audit, or reply "later" to defer

**Personalization points:**
- {{brand_name}} — their brand name
- {{first_name}} — their first name
- {{audit_target}} — same audit scope referenced in earlier emails

**Internal notes:**
The breakup email works because it removes pressure. The "later" option gives them a low-effort way to stay in the pipeline without committing now. The P.S. gets read even when the body gets skimmed — it's the highest-converting line in the email. Keep the tone warm and peer-to-peer. No guilt, no scarcity tricks. Confidence is the closer here.
