# Outreach Sequence A: Free Audit Offer

> **Purpose:** 3-email cold outreach sequence that leads with a free content audit of the prospect's existing emails, landing pages, or ads. The audit demonstrates our expertise and creates a natural bridge to a paid engagement.
> **Used by:** Brad for cold outreach to DTC brands with weak content that has visible room for improvement
> **Ideal prospect:** $1M–$10M DTC brand with an existing email/content program that has clear gaps — generic product descriptions, weak subject lines, missing flows, low-converting pages

---

## Sequence Overview

| Email | Send Day | Goal |
|-------|----------|------|
| 1 — The Observation | Day 1 | Earn a reply by proving you actually looked at their stuff |
| 2 — The Free Audit Offer | Day 4 | Convert curiosity into a yes for the free audit |
| 3 — The Breakup | Day 10 | Final nudge — leave the door open, move on |

---

## Email 1 — The Observation (Day 1)

**Goal:** Show you did your homework. Call out one specific, fixable content problem. No pitch yet — just earn credibility and a reply.

### Subject Lines (pick one)
1. `{{first_name}}, quick note on your welcome flow`
2. `noticed something on {{brand_name}}'s site`
3. `{{brand_name}}'s emails — one thought`

### Body

> Hi {{first_name}},
>
> I was looking at {{brand_name}} and noticed {{specific_observation}}.
>
> It's a small thing, but for a brand at your level it's probably leaving real money on the table. {{one_sentence_explaining_why}}.
>
> Not trying to sell you anything — just thought it was worth flagging. Happy to share the specifics if you're curious.
>
> — Brad

### Personalization Points
- **{{specific_observation}}** — Pick ONE concrete issue from their site, emails, or ads. Examples:
  - "your welcome email is text-only and doesn't mention your best-seller by name"
  - "your abandoned cart flow is a single email with no urgency or incentive"
  - "your product pages lean on feature specs but don't tell the story behind the product"
  - "your post-purchase sequence is missing — nothing lands after the order confirmation"
- **{{one_sentence_explaining_why}}** — Connect the observation to revenue. Examples:
  - "Welcome flows with product-specific hooks convert 2–3x better than generic ones"
  - "Most brands recover 5–10% of abandoned carts with a proper 3-email sequence"

### Internal Notes
- Keep this under 80 words. Short = credible. Long = salesy.
- Never criticize — frame everything as an opportunity, not a failure.
- The CTA is soft on purpose. You want a reply, not a meeting.
- If they reply with even mild curiosity ("sure, what did you notice?"), move directly to offering the audit.

## Email 2 — The Free Audit Offer (Day 4)

**Goal:** Offer the free content audit. Make it feel like a no-brainer — zero risk, high value, no commitment required.

### Subject Lines (pick one)
1. `free audit for {{brand_name}}?`
2. `re: {{brand_name}}'s content`
3. `worth 15 minutes?`

### Body

> Hi {{first_name}},
>
> Following up on my note — I actually went ahead and started pulling together a quick content audit for {{brand_name}}.
>
> I looked at {{two_or_three_things_you_reviewed}} and found a few areas where small copy changes could move the needle on revenue. For example, {{one_specific_quick_win}}.
>
> I'd love to walk you through the full audit — it's free, takes 15 minutes, and you'll walk away with at least 2–3 things you can implement that week whether or not we ever work together.
>
> Want me to send it over?
>
> — Brad

### Personalization Points
- **{{two_or_three_things_you_reviewed}}** — Name the specific assets you looked at. Examples:
  - "your welcome sequence, your top product page, and your last three email campaigns"
  - "your abandoned cart emails and your homepage hero copy"
- **{{one_specific_quick_win}}** — Give away one real insight to prove the audit has teeth. Examples:
  - "your cart abandonment email subject line is 'You left something behind' — that's what 90% of Shopify stores send. A line like 'Still thinking about the [product name]?' pulls 30–40% higher opens"
  - "your product page leads with ingredients before benefits — flipping that order alone can lift add-to-cart rates"

### Internal Notes
- The phrase "whether or not we ever work together" is load-bearing — it de-risks the ask.
- "Want me to send it over?" is the lowest-friction CTA possible. Not "book a call" — just "want it?"
- If they say yes, deliver the audit as a short Loom video (under 10 minutes) or a clean one-page PDF. Loom is preferred — it's more personal and harder to ignore.
- Target: 50% of opens should convert to replies. If not, the quick win isn't specific enough.

## Email 3 — The Breakup (Day 10)

**Goal:** Close the loop without being needy. Leave them with something useful so you stay top-of-mind if timing is wrong now.

### Subject Lines (pick one)
1. `closing the loop`
2. `last note from me`
3. `one last thing for {{brand_name}}`

### Body

> Hi {{first_name}},
>
> I know you're busy so I'll keep this short — I won't keep following up.
>
> If you ever want a second set of eyes on your emails, landing pages, or product copy, the offer stands. I work exclusively with DTC brands and I genuinely enjoy nerding out on this stuff.
>
> Either way, here's one thing you can do this week: {{parting_tip}}. No strings attached.
>
> Wishing you a great Q{{current_quarter}}.
>
> — Brad

### Personalization Points
- **{{parting_tip}}** — A specific, immediately actionable tip tied to their brand. Examples:
  - "A/B test your welcome email subject line — swap the generic 'Welcome to {{brand_name}}' for something that names your hero product. Takes 5 minutes and I've seen it lift open rates 15–25%"
  - "Add a single line of social proof to your cart page — even just '2,000+ happy customers' can reduce abandonment"
  - "Send a plain-text email to your list this week. No images, no design — just a short note from the founder about why you started the brand. These consistently outperform designed emails for DTC brands"

### Internal Notes
- This email builds long-term brand. Many prospects reply to breakup emails weeks or months later.
- The parting tip should be genuinely useful — it's your last impression.
- "I won't keep following up" creates relief and paradoxically makes them more likely to engage.
- Do NOT add a CTA to book a call. The softness is the strategy.

## Research Checklist (Before Starting the Sequence)

Before sending Email 1, find and document the following for each prospect:

- [ ] **Website** — Homepage, top 2–3 product pages, About page
- [ ] **Email signup flow** — Sign up for their list, screenshot the popup and welcome email(s)
- [ ] **Abandoned cart** — Add a product to cart, leave, and document the recovery emails
- [ ] **Recent campaigns** — Check inbox for any broadcast emails over the past 2 weeks
- [ ] **Ads** — Search Facebook Ad Library for active ads
- [ ] **Social** — Skim their Instagram for brand voice and positioning cues

Pick the single weakest area as your lead observation for Email 1.

---

## When to Use This Sequence

- **Best for:** DTC brands doing $1M–$10M with existing email/content programs that have clear room for improvement
- **Best signals:** Generic welcome email, missing post-purchase flow, bland product copy, default Klaviyo templates
- **Lead sources:** Shopify store directories, Facebook Ad Library, competitor customer lists, BuiltWith/Store Leads
- **Do NOT use when:** The brand's content is already strong — use Sequence B (Competitor Gap) or Sequence C (Results-Led) instead

## Success Metrics

- Target open rate: 45%+
- Target reply rate: 8–12%
- Target audit-to-proposal conversion: 50%+
- Target proposal-to-close rate: 25%+
