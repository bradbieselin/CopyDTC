# Quality Checklist

> **Purpose:** Pre-delivery checklist that every piece of content must pass before being sent to a client. This document ensures consistency, quality, and brand alignment across all deliverables produced by CopyDTC.
>
> **Used by:** Brad (and future content producers) before delivering any content to a client. Run through the relevant sections for every deliverable — no exceptions.

---

## How to Use This Checklist

Work through this checklist in order for every deliverable before it leaves your hands. Start with the Universal Checks, which apply to everything. Then move to the content-type-specific section that matches what you're delivering. Finish with the Final Sign-Off section.

A single unchecked item is a reason to pause and fix before sending. If you genuinely believe a check doesn't apply to a specific deliverable, note why in the delivery notes — don't just skip it silently.

---

## 1. Universal Checks (Apply to Every Deliverable)

These checks are non-negotiable. Every piece of content — email, ad, blog post, landing page, social caption — must clear all of these before moving to the content-type-specific checks.

### Brand Voice Alignment

- [ ] **Brand voice match confirmed.** Read the deliverable out loud and compare it against the client's Brand Voice Bible. The content should sound like the brand wrote it, not like a copywriter wrote it for them. Pay attention to sentence length, vocabulary level, and personality markers defined in the voice document.

- [ ] **Tone is appropriate for the channel.** A brand can have one voice but many tones. An email nurturing a warm lead should feel different from a retargeting ad for a cold audience, even for the same brand. Confirm that the tone shifts correctly for the specific channel and audience stage.

- [ ] **Words-to-avoid list checked — none present.** Many clients have specific words or phrases they never want associated with their brand. Pull up the client's words-to-avoid list (stored in their brand folder) and do a manual search or Ctrl+F through the deliverable. A single flagged word can undermine client trust.

- [ ] **Words-to-use list checked — key terms included.** The flip side of the avoid list. Clients often want specific terminology, branded phrases, or preferred descriptors woven into their content. Confirm these appear naturally — don't force them in, but make sure they're present where they fit.

- [ ] **No competitor names used negatively or incorrectly.** If a competitor is mentioned, it should be done strategically and accurately. Most of the time, avoid naming competitors altogether unless the content strategy explicitly calls for comparison positioning. When in doubt, leave them out.

### Copy Quality

- [ ] **No spelling or grammar errors.** Run the content through a spell-checker, but do not rely on it alone. Read the copy manually, word by word. Spell-checkers miss homophones (their/there/they're), awkward phrasing, and context-dependent errors. This is the most basic quality signal — a single typo tells the client you didn't care enough to proofread.

- [ ] **No placeholder text remaining.** Search the entire deliverable for brackets, placeholder tokens, and dummy text. Common culprits include `[BRAND_NAME]`, `[PRODUCT]`, `[CTA_LINK]`, `[INSERT STAT]`, `XX%`, and `Lorem ipsum`. Every variable and placeholder must be replaced with real, final content before delivery.

- [ ] **Word and character counts are within spec.** Every content type has length requirements, either from the client brief or from platform constraints. Verify counts before delivery. An email subject line that's 80 characters won't display properly. A social caption that exceeds the platform limit gets truncated. Check the brief, then check the content.

### Strategic Alignment

- [ ] **At least one specific product or service mention (where applicable).** Generic copy that could apply to any brand is a missed opportunity. Unless the content is purely brand-awareness or top-of-funnel, it should reference specific products, services, features, or offers that are unique to the client.

- [ ] **CTA is clear, specific, and actionable.** Every deliverable needs a call to action that tells the reader exactly what to do next. "Learn more" is weak. "Shop the Spring Collection" or "Book Your Free Consultation" tells the reader what they get and what action to take. Verify that the CTA matches the campaign goal and that there is a logical next step for the reader.

- [ ] **Content matches the brief and campaign objective.** Step back and ask: does this deliverable actually accomplish what the brief asked for? If the brief said "drive demo sign-ups," but the copy focuses on brand storytelling with no conversion mechanism, it misses the mark regardless of how well-written it is.

### Formatting and Delivery Readiness

- [ ] **Formatting is clean and ready to paste or upload.** The client or their team will be taking this content and putting it into an email platform, CMS, ad manager, or social scheduler. Remove stray line breaks, double spaces, inconsistent bullet formatting, and any artifacts from your drafting process. The content should look polished and professional the moment the client opens the file.

- [ ] **File naming follows the convention.** Name the deliverable file according to the naming convention defined in the delivery system documentation. Consistent file naming makes it easy for clients to find content and for you to track what's been delivered.

- [ ] **Correct deliverable format.** Confirm you're delivering in the format the client expects — Google Doc, PDF, plain text in an email, Notion page, or whatever was agreed upon. Don't make the client convert file types.

## 2. Email-Specific Checks

Apply these in addition to the Universal Checks whenever the deliverable includes email content (newsletters, promotional emails, nurture sequences, transactional email copy, etc.).

### Subject Line

- [ ] **Subject line is under 50 characters (ideal) or 60 characters maximum.** Most email clients truncate subject lines beyond 60 characters, especially on mobile. Shorter subject lines tend to outperform longer ones because they're fully visible in the inbox. Count the characters, not the words — punctuation, spaces, and emojis all count.

- [ ] **Subject line creates a reason to open.** A subject line has one job: get the email opened. It should spark curiosity, promise a benefit, create urgency, or feel personal. Evaluate whether the subject line would make you stop scrolling in a crowded inbox. If it reads like a label ("March Newsletter"), rewrite it.

- [ ] **Subject line avoids spam triggers.** Certain words and patterns increase the likelihood of landing in spam or promotions folders. Avoid ALL CAPS, excessive exclamation marks (!!!), and known trigger words like "free," "act now," "limited time" in isolation. Context matters — these words can be used strategically, but don't let them dominate the subject line.

### Preview Text

- [ ] **Preview text is 40-90 characters and compelling.** Preview text (preheader) appears after or below the subject line in most email clients. It's a second chance to earn the open. It should complement the subject line, not repeat it. If the subject line asks a question, the preview text can hint at the answer.

- [ ] **Preview text doesn't default to "View in browser" or body copy.** If preview text isn't explicitly set, most email platforms will pull the first line of the email body, which is often a utility link. Always write intentional preview text so the client's email looks professional in the inbox.

### Body Copy

- [ ] **Body copy uses short paragraphs — 2 to 3 sentences maximum.** Long paragraphs in email are unreadable, especially on mobile where screens are narrow. Break up the copy into scannable chunks. One idea per paragraph. Use white space generously.

- [ ] **Single clear CTA per email (primary).** Every email should drive the reader toward one primary action. Multiple competing CTAs dilute the message and reduce click-through rates. If there are secondary links (social profiles, blog links), they should be visually subordinate to the primary CTA button.

- [ ] **CTA button text is specific, not generic.** The CTA button should use action-oriented text that tells the reader what happens when they click. "Get My Free Guide" outperforms "Click Here." "Start My Trial" outperforms "Submit." Match the CTA text to the offer.

- [ ] **Dynamic tokens and personalization are properly formatted.** If the email uses merge tags or dynamic content (like `{{first_name}}`, `|FNAME|`, or platform-specific syntax), verify they are formatted correctly for the client's email platform. Test that fallback values are in place for contacts missing that data — "Hey there" instead of "Hey {{first_name}}" displaying literally.

- [ ] **Unsubscribe and compliance language noted.** While the client's email platform typically handles CAN-SPAM/GDPR compliance, flag any compliance requirements in your delivery notes. If the email requires specific legal disclaimers, terms-and-conditions links, or opt-out language, confirm they're included or note them clearly for the client's team to add.

## 3. Landing Page-Specific Checks

Apply these in addition to the Universal Checks when delivering landing page copy, sales pages, or dedicated conversion pages.

### Above the Fold

- [ ] **Hero headline is 8 words or fewer.** The headline is the first thing visitors see and the primary factor in whether they stay or bounce. Keep it short, benefit-driven, and immediately clear about what the page offers. Test it by asking: if someone read only this headline, would they understand the value proposition?

- [ ] **Subheadline expands on the headline with specificity.** The subheadline supports the headline with additional detail — who it's for, what they get, or how it works. It should be 1-2 sentences maximum and add information the headline alone can't convey.

- [ ] **Above-the-fold CTA is present and visible.** Visitors who are ready to convert should not need to scroll to take action. Include a clear CTA button in the above-the-fold section. This doesn't replace lower CTAs — it ensures high-intent visitors can act immediately.

### Page Body

- [ ] **Benefits-focused, not features-focused.** Features describe what something does. Benefits describe what the customer gets. "256-bit encryption" is a feature. "Your data stays private and secure" is a benefit. Lead with benefits, then support with features where needed. This is one of the most common copywriting mistakes — catch it here.

- [ ] **Social proof is included and specific.** Testimonials, case studies, review counts, client logos, or data points that demonstrate credibility. Generic claims like "trusted by thousands" are weak. Specific proof like "4.8 stars from 2,300+ reviews" or a named testimonial with a headshot builds real trust.

- [ ] **FAQ section addresses top objections.** Every product or service has 3-5 common objections that prevent people from buying. The FAQ section is where you neutralize these objections. Common ones include price justification, how it works, who it's for, guarantee/refund policies, and time commitment. Write answers that reassure, not just inform.

- [ ] **Image and video placement notes included.** As a copywriter, you're not designing the page, but you should indicate where visual elements belong. Note where hero images, product photos, explainer videos, or trust badges should appear. This helps the design team build a page that matches the copy's flow.

### Conversion and Readability

- [ ] **Mobile readability considered.** More than half of web traffic is mobile. Use short paragraphs, scannable subheadings, bullet points for lists, and avoid wide blocks of text. Read the copy on your phone (or simulate a mobile view) to confirm it's easy to consume on a small screen.

- [ ] **Page has a logical flow from awareness to action.** The page should guide the reader through a persuasion sequence: hook with the headline, explain the value, prove it with evidence, handle objections, and close with a CTA. If a section feels out of order or the page loses momentum, restructure before delivery.

- [ ] **Form fields (if applicable) are minimal and justified.** If the landing page includes a form, every field should earn its place. Each additional field reduces conversion rates. Name and email are standard. Anything beyond that — phone number, company name, job title — should be there because the sales process genuinely requires it, not "just in case."

## 4. Blog-Specific Checks

Apply these in addition to the Universal Checks when delivering blog posts, articles, or long-form content pieces.

### SEO Fundamentals

- [ ] **Meta title is 50-60 characters and includes the primary keyword.** The meta title is what appears in search engine results and browser tabs. It must include the target keyword, ideally near the beginning, and stay within the character limit so it doesn't get truncated in SERPs.

- [ ] **Meta description is 150-160 characters and compelling.** The meta description appears below the title in search results. It should summarize the post's value, include the primary keyword naturally, and give searchers a reason to click. Write it like ad copy — it's competing for attention on the results page.

- [ ] **Primary keyword appears 4-6 times naturally throughout the post.** Keyword usage should feel organic, not forced. Aim for the primary keyword in the title, intro paragraph, at least two body sections, and the conclusion. If inserting the keyword makes a sentence awkward, rewrite the sentence rather than stuffing the keyword in.

- [ ] **Secondary and related keywords are incorporated.** Beyond the primary keyword, include semantically related terms and long-tail variations that support the topic. This signals topical depth to search engines and captures a wider range of search queries.

### Structure and Readability

- [ ] **H2/H3 heading structure is logical and scannable.** Readers skim blog posts before deciding to read. Headings should form a coherent outline of the article — someone reading only the headings should understand the post's key points. Use H2 for main sections and H3 for subsections within them.

- [ ] **Introduction hooks the reader within the first 2-3 sentences.** The intro should immediately establish relevance: what problem does this post solve, what will the reader learn, or why should they care? Avoid long windups and generic opening lines like "In today's fast-paced world..."

- [ ] **Paragraphs are 3-4 sentences maximum.** Long paragraphs are intimidating on screen. Break ideas into digestible chunks. Use transition words to maintain flow between short paragraphs so the post still reads smoothly.

- [ ] **Conclusion includes a clear next step or CTA.** Every blog post should guide the reader to an action — subscribe, download a resource, read a related post, try a product, or contact the business. Don't let the post just trail off.

### Linking and Media

- [ ] **Internal linking opportunities identified.** Note where links to other content on the client's site should go. Internal links improve SEO, keep readers on the site longer, and guide them deeper into the funnel. Aim for 2-5 internal links per post, depending on length.

- [ ] **External source links included where claims are made.** If the post references statistics, studies, or third-party data, include source links. This builds credibility with readers and signals trustworthiness to search engines.

- [ ] **Image placement and alt text suggestions included.** Note where images, graphics, or screenshots should appear to break up text and illustrate key points. Include suggested alt text descriptions for each image — these support accessibility and contribute to image SEO.

## 5. Ad-Specific Checks

Apply these in addition to the Universal Checks when delivering ad copy for any platform (Meta, Google, TikTok, LinkedIn, etc.).

### Platform Compliance

- [ ] **Character limits respected for the target platform.** Every ad platform has strict character limits for headlines, descriptions, and body text. Exceeding them means the ad won't run or will be truncated. Reference the platform's current specs and count characters precisely — don't estimate.

  Common limits to verify:
  - **Meta (Facebook/Instagram):** Primary text 125 characters visible (up to 1,000 total), Headline 40 characters, Description 30 characters
  - **Google Search:** Headlines 30 characters each (up to 15), Descriptions 90 characters each (up to 4)
  - **Google Display:** Headlines 30 characters, Long headline 90 characters, Description 90 characters
  - **LinkedIn:** Intro text 150 characters visible (up to 600 total), Headline 70 characters
  - **TikTok:** Ad text 100 characters visible, Display name 40 characters

- [ ] **Ad copy complies with platform advertising policies.** Each platform prohibits certain claims, language, and tactics. Common restrictions include misleading claims, excessive capitalization, profanity, and making personal attribute assumptions ("Are you overweight?"). Review the relevant platform's ad policy if the content is in a sensitive category (health, finance, dating, etc.).

### Creative Effectiveness

- [ ] **Hook is in the first line.** The first line of ad copy determines whether someone stops scrolling or keeps going. It must be attention-grabbing and immediately relevant to the target audience. Lead with a pain point, a surprising claim, a question, or a bold statement — not with the brand name or a generic introduction.

- [ ] **Multiple variations provided.** Always deliver at least 3 variations of each ad — different hooks, angles, or structures targeting the same audience and objective. This gives the client material for A/B testing and prevents creative fatigue. Variations should be genuinely different approaches, not minor word swaps.

- [ ] **Each variation has a distinct angle or hook.** "Same ad with different adjectives" is not a variation. Each version should test a different psychological trigger: one might lead with social proof, another with a pain point, another with a benefit, and another with curiosity. Label each variation with its angle so the client understands the testing strategy.

- [ ] **Creative direction notes included.** Ad copy doesn't exist in isolation — it pairs with visuals or video. Include notes on what the visual should convey, suggested imagery styles, text overlay recommendations, and how the copy and creative work together. Even brief notes like "pair with before/after photo" or "use UGC-style video" give the client's design team valuable direction.

### Audience and Funnel Alignment

- [ ] **Copy matches the audience's awareness stage.** A cold audience ad should focus on the problem and build curiosity. A warm retargeting ad can be more direct about the offer. A hot audience ad can go straight to urgency and conversion. Confirm the copy matches the funnel stage indicated in the brief.

- [ ] **Platform-specific best practices followed.** Different platforms have different user behaviors and norms. LinkedIn ads should feel professional. TikTok ads should feel native and casual. Meta ads need to compete in a fast-scroll feed. Write for how people actually use each platform, not with a one-size-fits-all approach.

## 6. Social Media-Specific Checks

Apply these in addition to the Universal Checks when delivering organic social media content (posts, captions, stories, threads).

### Platform Fit

- [ ] **Captions feel native to the platform.** A LinkedIn post should not read like an Instagram caption and vice versa. LinkedIn favors professional storytelling, data, and insights. Instagram favors casual, visual-first language. Twitter/X demands brevity and punchiness. Write for the platform's culture, not just its character limits.

- [ ] **Hashtags are relevant, correctly formatted, and appropriately numbered.** Research hashtags for relevance and reach before including them. Use a mix of high-volume and niche hashtags. The right number varies by platform — Instagram allows up to 30 but 5-15 focused tags perform better; LinkedIn uses 3-5; Twitter/X uses 1-2 at most. Ensure no spaces or special characters break the hashtag.

- [ ] **Caption length is appropriate for the platform and content type.** Instagram carousels can support longer captions because the visual content holds attention. A simple photo post might warrant a shorter caption. Twitter/X threads have a different dynamic than single tweets. Match the caption length to both the platform norms and the specific content being posted.

### Engagement and Content Quality

- [ ] **Engagement prompt included.** Social media rewards interaction. Include a question, prompt, or invitation that encourages comments, shares, saves, or DMs. This can be direct ("Drop a fire emoji if you agree") or subtle ("What's your take?"). The prompt should feel natural, not forced.

- [ ] **Visual direction is specific and actionable.** Social copy is inseparable from its visual component. Don't write "include an eye-catching image" — instead write "flat-lay product photo on marble background with warm lighting" or "screenshot of the customer review highlighted in yellow." Specific direction produces better results and reduces revision cycles.

- [ ] **Post fits within the content calendar or campaign strategy.** If the client has a content calendar or series structure, confirm that this post aligns with the planned theme, timing, and messaging sequence. A standalone post that contradicts or repeats a recent post undermines the overall social strategy.

- [ ] **Accessibility considerations addressed.** Suggest alt text for images, recommend camelCase for multi-word hashtags (#SmallBusinessTips vs #smallbusinesstips) for screen reader compatibility, and avoid relying on emojis to convey critical meaning. Inclusive content reaches a wider audience and reflects well on the brand.

## 7. Final Sign-Off

This is the gut-check section. After passing all the technical checks above, step back and evaluate the deliverable as a whole.

- [ ] **Would I be proud to show this in our portfolio?** This is the quality bar. If you would hesitate to include this piece in a portfolio of your best work, it's not ready. Identify what's holding it back and fix it.

- [ ] **Does this meet the "ready to send" standard?** Not "ready for another draft" or "good enough for now" — genuinely ready for the client to use immediately. The client should be able to take this content and deploy it without needing to fix, rewrite, or question anything.

- [ ] **Have I reviewed this with fresh eyes?** If you wrote this in one sitting, take a break — even 15 minutes — before running this checklist. Reviewing your own work immediately after writing it causes you to read what you intended to write rather than what's actually on the page. Fresh eyes catch errors that tired eyes miss.

- [ ] **Has this been reviewed by a second set of eyes (when possible)?** A second reviewer catches things the original writer is blind to — unclear phrasing, logical gaps, tone inconsistencies, and simple typos. When a second review isn't possible due to timeline, apply extra rigor to the self-review steps above.

- [ ] **Delivery notes are complete.** Include any context the client needs alongside the content: assumptions you made, questions that need client input before publishing, strategic rationale for creative choices, and anything the client's team needs to know to implement the content correctly.
